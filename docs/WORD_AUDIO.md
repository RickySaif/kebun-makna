# Word audio: how the clips were cut, and what changed

Generated by `beta/tools/recut-words.mjs` from `beta/tools/word-boundaries.json`. Every word clip in `beta/audio/w/` is cut from Alafasy's own ayah recitation (`audio/a/`), never TTS. The live game's `audio/` folder is untouched; at cutover `beta/audio/w/` replaces `audio/w/`.

## Why
The old boundaries were placed by hand. In 67 of 201 word clips at least one edge moved by more than 80 ms. Example: 114:1 "aʿūdhu" was cut at 1.03 s, in the middle of its "dhu", so its card played "aʿū" and the "dhu" played on the next card, "birabbi". Example: 114:5 "ṣudūri" ended before its "ri", which then played on the next card.

## How the new boundaries were found
1. **Two independent sources.** The old hand-placed boundaries, and Quran.com's published word timings for the same Alafasy recording (the file there has exactly the same length as ours, for all 48 ayat).
2. **Agreement.** For 64 of 153 boundaries the two sources agree within 80 ms. Each was moved to the strongest change of sound inside that range, and all of them sit on a clear change (closure, burst, hiss or new vowel).
3. **Disagreement.** For the other 89 boundaries each one was checked by eye on a spectrogram, and neither source was trusted blindly. Neither is always right: Quran.com often ends a word in the middle of its long vowel and gives the rest of the vowel to the next word (e.g. "yadā | abī"), and the old cuts often fall inside a syllable (e.g. inside the "sh" of "sharri").
4. **Rules used when reading the spectrogram.** A boundary goes where the next word's first sound begins. Before a stop (b, d, t, k, ṭ) or a glottal stop it is the middle of the silence after the vowel. Before a hiss (sh, s, ṣ, kh, ḥ, j) it is where the noise starts. After a long vowel the whole vowel stays with its own word. Where a nasal hum joins two words (idgham or ikhfa' with ghunna), the boundary sits inside the hum so both words keep their nasal.
5. Clips get a 6 ms fade-in and a 12 ms fade-out to avoid clicks, and are encoded like the originals (mono, 44.1 kHz, 64 kbps).

## What is still uncertain
This was checked by signal analysis and spectrograms, not by listening. The least certain boundaries are the long nasal hums: 105:4 (hijāratin | min | sijjīl), 105:5 (ka'aṣfin | ma'kūlin), 106:4 (all five), 111:5 (ḥablun | min | masad), and 107:4 (fawaylun | lil-muṣallīn). Please listen to these with `beta/cek-audio.html`.
